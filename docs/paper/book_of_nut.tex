\begin{abstract}
The Book of Nut, painted on the ceilings of New Kingdom royal tombs, describes the sun's journey across the body of the sky goddess---swallowed at dusk, traveling through her body at night, and reborn at dawn. Standard Egyptology reads this as mythological astronomy. This document re-reads the Book of Nut line by line through the Egyptian embedding space---10,833 ancient Egyptian words projected into 300-dimensional GloVe English space via Ridge regression. For each key passage, we compare the standard translation against what the geometry of the language itself reveals. The line-by-line analysis shows that Nut is not sky but time; that the Duat (underworld) is a solar concept, not a chthonic one; that night is a temporal position rather than a visual condition; and that the nocturnal journey of Ra is, in the language's own geometry, a process of transformation within time, not a transit through darkness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Book of Nut (\textit{Nut Fundamentaltext}) survives in several New Kingdom royal tombs, most completely in the cenotaph of Seti I at Abydos (c.\,1280 BCE) and the tomb of Ramesses IV (KV 2). It describes the mechanics of the cosmos: how the sun enters Nut's mouth at the western horizon, travels through her body during the twelve hours of night, and is born from her at dawn in the east. The stars follow a similar cycle. The text is accompanied by a monumental image of Nut arched over the earth, her body studded with stars, Shu supporting her from below.

Every translation treats the Book of Nut as a work of mythological astronomy---a pre-scientific account of celestial mechanics dressed in divine narrative. The question this document asks is whether the Egyptian language itself supports this reading, or whether the astronomical framework is a modern projection.

\subsection{Method}

Each Egyptian word in the key passages was looked up in the V15 aligned embedding space (10,833 words projected into GloVe 300d). For each word, we record the nearest English neighbors and the nearest Egyptian neighbors, identifying where these mappings diverge from the standard translation's implied framework. The passages below follow the text as reconstructed by \citet{vonlieven2007book} and \citet{neugebauer1960ean}.

\subsection{How to Read the Line-by-Line Analysis}

Each passage is presented in three layers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item {\color{passage}\textbf{The Egyptian passage}} in hieroglyphic script with transliteration, and the standard English translation.
    \item \textbf{Word-level geometry}: Key words are looked up in the embedding space. English neighbors (what the word maps to in GloVe space) and Egyptian neighbors (what the word clusters with in the native vocabulary) are reported.
    \item {\color{insight}\textbf{Geometric reading}}: What the passage says when the embedding-space meanings replace the conventional translations.
\end{enumerate}

{\color{insight}\textbf{Geometric reading:}} The opening line identifies Nut with the sky, but the two words occupy entirely different semantic regions. \textit{P,t} maps cleanly to ``sky'' (0.86)---spatial, visual, blue, bright. \textit{Nw,t} maps to ``time'' (0.59)---temporal, sequential, processual. Either the text is making an equation the language doesn't support, or the passage means something closer to: ``This Time---she is the great expanse.''

\section{General Analysis}
\label{sec:general}

The line-by-line analysis reveals a systematic pattern: wherever the standard translation assumes spatial-astronomical reference, the embedding space yields temporal-processual meaning. This section synthesizes the findings.

\subsection{Nut Is Time, Not Sky}

The most consequential finding is the geometric separation between \textit{Nw,t} (time: 0.59) and \textit{p,t} (sky: 0.86). These two words occupy entirely different regions of semantic space. \textit{P,t} maps to spatial-visual concepts---blue, bright, above. \textit{Nw,t} maps to temporal concepts---when, before, come.

This means the Book of Nut is not a text about the sky. It is a text about time. The monumental image of Nut arched over the earth is not a diagram of the physical cosmos; it is a depiction of temporal experience---the surface across which all events unfold. The stars on her body are not objects in a sky; they are events in time.

\subsection{The Duat as Dawn-Workshop}

The Duat (\textit{dwꜣ,t}) shares its root and its geometry with morning (\textit{dwꜣ,w}). Both map to ``come'' and ``next.'' The Western concept of an underworld as a dark, subterranean realm has no geometric support in Egyptian space. The Duat is a solar concept---the place where dawn is generated. The Book of Nut's description of Ra traveling through Nut at night is not a journey through darkness but a journey through the \textit{place where morning is produced}.

The Amduat's description of the nocturnal hours as brightly lit, populated by fire-breathing serpents and blazing lakes, is consistent with this reading. The Duat is not dark because it is not an underworld---it is a solar-temporal workshop.

\subsection{Night as Temporal Position}

Night (\textit{grḥ}) maps to sequential terms---``came,'' ``last,'' ``first''---not to darkness or the absence of light. The Egyptian night is not a visual condition but a position in a cycle. This reframes the entire nocturnal journey: Ra does not pass through darkness but through a sequence of temporal positions, each with its own character and intensity.

\subsection{Sunrise as Re-Creation}

The compound \textit{sṯz,w-Šw} (the lifting of Shu) appears in the neighborhood of \textit{wbn} (sunrise, 0.77). Every sunrise recapitulates the original creation---the separation of time from earth. The Book of Nut does not describe a daily routine; it describes a daily re-creation. Shu is not atmosphere holding up a sky-vault; Shu is the structural force that maintains the distinction between temporal and material existence. Each dawn, that distinction must be re-established.

\subsection{The Horizon as Threshold of Power}

The horizon (\textit{ꜣḫ,t}) neighbors \textit{nb-ḫpš} (lord of strength, 0.95), \textit{nb-tm} (lord of totality, 0.94), and \textit{ḥw,t.pl} (temples, 0.95). It is not a geographical feature but a zone of maximum cosmic intensity. This explains why the horizon-glyph is used in the names of temples and royal monuments: they participate in the horizon's quality as a threshold of total power, not a line on a landscape.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The Book of Nut as Temporal Treatise}

If the readings above are correct, the Book of Nut is not a work of mythological astronomy. It is a treatise on the nature of time---how time functions, what happens within time, how entities persist and transform within temporal experience, and how the original creation (the separation of time from matter) is renewed with each sunrise.

This reading resolves several long-standing puzzles:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Why is Nut depicted swallowing and birthing the sun?} Because time consumes and regenerates events. The sun does not move through space; it is consumed by time and re-emerged by time.

    \item \textbf{Why are the decans on Nut's body?} Because they are temporal events, not astronomical objects. They mark the structure of time, not the positions of stars.

    \item \textbf{Why is the Duat not dark?} Because it is the place of the dawn---the temporal workshop where morning is generated. The Amduat's description of the nocturnal hours as brightly lit makes sense if the underworld is a solar-temporal space.

    \item \textbf{Why does the Book of Nut appear on tomb ceilings?} The dead enter Nut---they enter time. The tomb ceiling is not a sky map but a temporal map, showing the deceased the structure of the eternity they are about to inhabit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implications}

The Nut-as-time hypothesis is testable. If correct, passages in the Book of Nut that have resisted coherent spatial-astronomical translation should yield more coherent temporal translations. Passages that describe the ``distance'' between stars should read better as the ``duration'' between events. Passages that describe Nut's body as a ``vault'' should read better as a ``span'' (of time). This is a target for future geometric analysis of the full Book of Nut text.
